Challenge

Help patients feel informed before they reach out

Create a clear vision correction website that answers patient questions, builds trust in the clinic and helps turn interest into consultation requests.

Choosing eye surgery is personal. The website answers visitors’ questions step by step and helps them feel confident about booking a consultation.

“Where do I start?”

Several procedures and unfamiliar terms can make the first decision feel overwhelming.

“What happens next?”

Visitors need a clear picture of the consultation, procedure and recovery.

“Who can I trust?”

The people, equipment and follow-up care should be easy to find.

“Can I ask someone?”

The next step should stay visible when a visitor is ready to talk.

Process

A journey built around patient questions

I mapped the page as a sequence of questions: what are my options, what happens, who will care for me, and how do I take the next step?

Question→Options→Procedure→Specialists→Recovery→Consultation

Illustrative layout studies reconstructed from the finished page.

The direction became clear: explain one decision at a time, give the medical team a human presence, and keep the next step easy to find.

Key decisions

Less uncertainty, more clarity

A familiar visual language and a simpler reading path help visitors understand what the clinic offers before they decide to get in touch.

01

Start with the patient's question

The opening screen asks which vision correction method might be right for the visitor, with one clear way to begin.

02

Make the options comparable

SMILE, flap-based LASIK and surface ablation are grouped together so the differences can be scanned without searching the page.

03

Turn a long process into steps

Before, during and after surgery are presented as separate stages, making the sequence easier to follow.

04

Put people before technology

Specialist profiles, names and experience bring a human face to a medical decision. Equipment details remain available for those who want them.

05

A quiz that opens the conversation

A six-question experience helps visitors reflect on their vision. It points to a consultation rather than making a diagnosis.

06

Comfortable on a small screen

The mobile version stacks information into readable sections, keeps touch targets generous and makes contact points easy to reach.

Design

A calm visual system for a personal decision

The site pairs clean medical photography with soft gray surfaces, generous white space and the clinic's recognizable teal.
